## Adaptive Creature Rendering Engine (STRICT & FIXED — DO NOT MODIFY)

Create a stylized painterly fantasy creature splash illustration for an original narrative role-playing game.

Show the complete creature silhouette by default, including every intentional head, limb, wing, tail, horn, appendage, and contact point. Leave breathing room around the outermost anatomy.

The creature must be designed from its stated body plan rather than forced into human proportions.

The image must combine:

* simplified and authored anatomy;
* large geometric masses;
* a readable center of gravity;
* one clear action line;
* controlled painterly brushwork;
* strong silhouette separation;
* one adaptive identity focus;
* one restrained narrative effect;
* a recognizable but simplified environment.

The result should feel like original fantasy game splash art: expressive, shape-driven, moderately exaggerated, and designed rather than observed.

Favor graphic proportion, silhouette rhythm, and large color planes over zoological realism, photographic rendering, or dense surface information.

Do not turn the creature into a complete battle scene.

Do not add hunters, adventuring parties, prey, armies, or background characters unless explicitly requested.

---

## Adaptive Composition Format

Use an explicitly requested aspect ratio when provided.

When no aspect ratio is supplied, choose according to the dominant silhouette:

* **9:16 Vertical:** Upright humanoids, liches, goblins, giants, werebeasts, tall spirits, and vertically elongated creatures.
* **4:5 Portrait:** Compact quadrupeds, heavy beasts, broad monsters, crouched predators, and creatures whose mass is more important than reach.
* **16:9 Landscape:** Dragons, serpentine creatures, flying creatures with wide wings, long-bodied monsters, and creatures defined by horizontal movement.
* **1:1 Square:** Radial, amorphous, coiled, many-limbed, shell-like, or nearly circular silhouettes.

The selected format must support the anatomy rather than compress it.

Never place a long dragon in a narrow vertical frame by default.

Never stretch a compact quadruped merely to fill a tall composition.

The complete creature should generally occupy 70–88% of the useful frame along its longest silhouette axis, with extra margin around wings, horns, tail tips, and active effects.

## Body Plan First

Establish the body plan before surface detail.

Determine:

1. number of heads;
2. number and type of limbs;
3. exact limb attachment points;
4. upright, quadrupedal, serpentine, radial, amorphous, or mixed posture;
5. center of gravity;
6. primary locomotion;
7. weight-bearing contacts;
8. major counterbalances such as tail, wings, neck, shell, or abdomen.

Every visible appendage must connect clearly to a major body mass.

Do not invent extra limbs, wings, tails, heads, horns, eyes, or joints beyond the description.

Do not remove required anatomy because of the pose or framing.

When a creature has unusual anatomy, prioritize structural clarity over visual complexity.

---

## Proportion Language

Describe and render proportions through relationships:

* head relative to torso;
* shoulder or chest width relative to hips;
* forelimb length relative to hindlimbs;
* leg length relative to body height;
* neck length relative to torso;
* tail length relative to total body length;
* wingspan relative to body length;
* horn, antler, or crest size relative to head;
* hand, claw, paw, hoof, or talon size relative to the supporting limb.

Use three to five dominant masses to establish the creature.

Examples of dominant masses include:

* head;
* ribcage or thorax;
* pelvis or rear body;
* wing pair;
* tail;
* shell;
* serpentine trunk;
* magical core.

Subtle exaggeration is encouraged when it strengthens identity:

* larger shoulders for force;
* longer forelimbs for stalking or climbing;
* compact hindquarters for stability;
* oversized wings for aerial dominance;
* elongated neck for elegance or menace;
* reduced head size for colossal scale;
* enlarged skull or eyes for supernatural intelligence.

Avoid realistic anatomical measurement when it weakens the intended design.

Avoid arbitrary exaggeration that breaks locomotion or weight support.

## Anatomy Coherence

Maintain traceable anatomy from each major joint to the end of the limb.

For bipeds:

* preserve clear shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle, and foot relationships;
* distinguish plantigrade, digitigrade, and hoofed leg structures;
* do not automatically give human hands or feet to nonhuman creatures.

For quadrupeds:

* preserve separate forelimb and hindlimb structures;
* keep the spine, shoulder mass, ribcage, pelvis, and weight distribution readable;
* do not turn the forelimbs into human arms unless explicitly hybrid.

For winged creatures:

* define whether wings are a separate limb pair or transformed forelimbs;
* attach wings to a believable shoulder, chest, or dorsal structure;
* keep both wings consistent in membrane, feather, bone, and joint pattern;
* distinguish folded, half-open, gliding, and fully extended states.

For serpentine or limbless creatures:

* use clear body taper, coil overlap, and ground contact;
* prevent loops from merging into impossible knots;
* use head direction and spinal curve as the main action line.

For many-limbed creatures:

* group limbs into readable pairs or rhythms;
* preserve a consistent attachment system;
* simplify overlaps before sacrificing limb count.

For amorphous or incorporeal creatures:

* define one stable identity core;
* preserve a readable outer silhouette;
* distinguish intentional fluidity from accidental anatomical melting.

## Center of Identity

The highest-detail area is the creature's declared center of identity.

This may be:

* eyes and face;
* skull;
* beak and facial disk;
* jaw and teeth;
* horn crown;
* sensory cluster;
* chest core;
* phylactery;
* mask;
* rune cavity;
* internal flame;
* another explicitly described feature.

Do not force a human facial hierarchy onto creatures without human faces.

Use the strongest local contrast and the sharpest selected edges around the center of identity.

If the creature intentionally wears a mask, helm, veil, or facial armor, treat that object as part of the identity focus rather than trying to reveal a human face beneath it.

---

## Head Construction

Build the head from two to four primary masses.

Keep skull, muzzle, beak, jaw, facial disk, eye placement, horns, ears, and neck connection structurally coherent.

Eyes should be simplified and expressive without becoming glossy anime eyes.

Teeth, fangs, and beak serrations should be grouped into clean shapes rather than individually rendered.

Horns, antlers, tusks, and crests should use a clear base, growth direction, taper, and silhouette.

Avoid:

* random extra eyes;
* duplicated jaws;
* disconnected horns;
* floating teeth;
* human lips on animal muzzles unless explicitly hybrid;
* excessive saliva;
* realistic gore;
* micro-detailed facial texture.

---

## Pose, Locomotion, and Action

Capture one action at its anticipation, midpoint, or aftermath.

Use the creature's actual locomotion and center of gravity.

Possible actions include:

* stalking;
* bracing;
* landing;
* taking flight;
* rearing;
* turning;
* charging;
* crawling;
* climbing;
* coiling;
* emerging;
* roaring;
* casting;
* commanding;
* defending territory;
* performing a ritual.

Use one dominant diagonal, curve, spiral, or triangular flow.

Keep the pose readable at thumbnail size.

Ensure that weight-bearing limbs visibly support the mass.

Use wings, tail, neck, cloak, smoke, leaves, ash, dust, sparks, or magic to continue the main gesture without creating conflicting directions.

Avoid:

* multiple simultaneous actions;
* extreme foreshortening;
* anatomy hidden by the effect;
* limbs merged into the torso;
* every limb raised from the ground without a valid airborne pose;
* impossible balance;
* theatrical symmetry;
* chaotic rage posing without behavioral specificity.

## Scale Communication

Communicate scale through environmental relationships rather than text labels.

Use one clear scale anchor such as:

* doorway;
* castle wall;
* ruined arch;
* tower;
* ancient tree;
* bridge;
* ship mast;
* cliff;
* standing stone;
* large statue;
* road;
* cave entrance.

For small creatures, use oversized grass, roots, stones, furniture, tools, or architectural steps.

For huge or colossal creatures, reduce head-to-body ratio, simplify distant surfaces, and use large environmental masses.

Do not add a tiny human silhouette solely for scale unless explicitly requested.

Do not make large creatures fill the frame so tightly that wings, tail, horns, or feet are cropped.

---

## Surface and Material Simplification

Treat every surface as a few large material masses.

* **Short Fur:** Broad matte planes with sparse directional edge breaks.
* **Long Fur:** Two or three flowing masses with limited tuft accents.
* **Feathers:** Grouped wing, crest, and body planes; never render every feather.
* **Scales:** Large directional scale zones suggested through value and a few edge marks.
* **Reptilian Skin:** Broad color planes with restrained texture.
* **Chitin or Shell:** Large hard planes with limited edge highlights.
* **Bone:** Warm ivory or grey-beige masses with restrained cracks.
* **Decay:** Subdued broken shapes without gore or exposed anatomical excess.
* **Stone or Crystal:** Large faceted planes with selective sharp edges.
* **Plant Matter:** Unified bark, moss, leaf, or root masses.
* **Mist, Shadow, or Flame:** One coherent flowing mass anchored to an identity core.
* **Metal and Armor:** Dull geometric plates with no mirror-like reflections.
* **Cloth and Leather:** Large worn shapes subordinate to anatomy.

Do not use texture to compensate for weak silhouette or anatomy.

---

## Intelligent Creatures and Equipment

Equipment is optional.

For animalistic creatures, omit clothing and tools unless narratively justified.

For sapient creatures, equipment should reveal culture, role, status, ritual, or history.

Use no more than two prominent items.

Equipment must fit the creature's anatomy:

* grips must match hands, claws, tentacles, jaws, or harness systems;
* armor must follow actual joints and body masses;
* saddles, collars, barding, robes, jewelry, and weapons must not imply unsupported human anatomy;
* worn items must not hide the body plan.

Avoid generic loot-display presentation, excessive belts, floating weapons, overloaded armor, and decorative clutter.

## Power and Narrative Effect

Use one primary visible effect tied to the declared power and action.

Possible effects include:

* one breath stream;
* one magical arc;
* one smoke trail;
* one ash or dust wake;
* one controlled aura around the identity core;
* one lightning branch system;
* one frost trail;
* one shadow extension;
* one necromantic flame mass;
* one displaced leaf or feather arc.

The effect must:

* follow the action line;
* preserve the body silhouette;
* avoid hiding attachment points;
* remain lower in detail than the identity focus;
* use the dominant accent color.

Avoid multiple powers, particle fields, giant spell circles, summoned armies, explosive spectacle, or effects covering the entire anatomy.

## Abstract Environmental Background

Build the environment from:

* one dominant structural anchor;
* no more than two contextual cues;
* one principal light source;
* foreground, creature layer, and background separation;
* broad atmospheric masses.

The environment should establish habitat and scale without becoming a complete scenic illustration.

Possible habitats include:

* ancient forest;
* mountain pass;
* ruined castle;
* crypt;
* cavern;
* swamp;
* volcanic ridge;
* frozen valley;
* coastal cliff;
* desert ruin;
* abandoned village;
* arcane sanctuary;
* battlefield aftermath;
* underground temple.

Keep background edges softer and lower in contrast than the creature.

Do not place high-contrast background shapes through the identity focus.

Do not add unrelated narrative action.

---

## Lighting and Color

Use one principal light source and, when useful, one subordinate rim-light accent.

Construct lighting with broad angular or curved value masses.

Keep shadows chromatic and readable.

Use moderately heightened contrast around the identity focus and action-facing anatomy.

Use a controlled palette:

* 60% dominant family;
* 30% supporting family;
* 10% accent family.

The accent may support:

* eyes;
* mouth or breath;
* magical core;
* power effect;
* ritual mark;
* selected crest, feather, scale, or equipment detail.

Avoid rainbow lighting, multiple bright sources, neon overload, glossy skin, broad bloom, or uniformly saturated color.

---

## Edge and Detail Hierarchy

Use the following hierarchy:

* **Identity focus:** 100% relative detail.
* **Head connection and action-leading anatomy:** 70%.
* **Primary limb, wing, jaw, weapon, or power source:** 55%.
* **Major torso and body masses:** 45%.
* **Secondary limbs, wings, and tail structure:** 35%.
* **Narrative effect:** 30%.
* **Scale anchor:** 25%.
* **Outer silhouette and ground contacts:** 20%.
* **Secondary environmental cues:** 12%.
* **Distant background:** 5–8%.

Reserve sharp edges for the identity focus, important joints, leading claws or talons, selected horn or wing edges, and one or two effect accents.

Use medium edges for major anatomy and ground contacts.

Use soft or lost edges for peripheral fur, feathers, tail tips, distant wing edges, atmosphere, and background.

Do not soften anatomy so much that limb count becomes ambiguous.

## Composition Checklist

Before finalizing, verify:

* the selected aspect ratio matches the silhouette;
* the full anatomy fits inside the frame;
* the limb, wing, head, and tail count matches the description;
* every appendage has a visible attachment;
* the center of gravity matches the pose;
* weight-bearing contacts are credible;
* the center of identity is obvious;
* one action dominates;
* only one narrative effect dominates;
* the environment communicates habitat and scale;
* no human anatomy has been added by default;
* the silhouette remains readable at thumbnail size.

* excessive gore;
* exposed organs;
* dense decay texture;
* numerous accessories;
* overdesigned armor;

* multiple magical effects;
* particle overload;
* complete battle scenes;
