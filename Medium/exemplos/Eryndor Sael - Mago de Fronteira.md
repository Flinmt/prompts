## Simplified Geometric Style Anchor

Every part of the illustration must follow these principles:

* **Style Signature:** "Stylized full-body fantasy character splash art for an original narrative game; expressive diagonal gesture; large geometric facial and body planes; simplified anatomy; authored proportions; economical brushwork; selective detail; controlled cinematic lighting; one restrained narrative effect; original fantasy costume language; recognizable abstract environment; unfinished outer edges."

* **Shape Economy:** "Use the fewest possible shapes needed to describe the character and location. Prefer one strong large shape over several small shapes."

* **Value Economy:** "Construct the figure and environment using three or four principal values: light, midtone, shadow, and a limited deep accent."

* **Color Economy:** "Use a narrow palette composed of related muted neutrals and no more than one moderately stronger dominant accent color tied to the action, magic, faith, or equipment."

* **Detail Economy:** "Render only information that supports the face, expression, silhouette, profession, social role, environmental identity, or essential character narrative."

* **Brush Economy:** "Each brushstroke should describe a plane, edge, shadow, structural direction, atmosphere, or material boundary. Avoid random decorative texture."

* **Costume Economy:** "Describe the costume through three to five major garment masses and no more than two prominent accessories."

* **Environment Economy:** "Describe the location through one dominant structural anchor and no more than two secondary contextual cues."

* **Silhouette Priority:** "The character's role and personality should remain readable from the silhouette alone, even at thumbnail size."

* **Environmental Readability:** "The location should be understandable at a glance without inviting close inspection of background detail."

## World Costume Direction

The clothing language belongs to an original medieval-fantasy world shaped by rival kingdoms, ancient faiths, dangerous wilderness, feudal obligations, mercenary companies, arcane traditions, trade routes, old ruins, and competing peoples.

The costume design should evoke the breadth of a tabletop fantasy role-playing world while remaining grounded, culturally specific, practical, and entirely original. It may support classic adventuring roles and varied ancestries without reproducing a recognizable character, faction, costume, or visual identity from an existing franchise.

The clothing must feel:

* culturally grounded and lived-in;
* practical but expressive of class, ancestry, faith, region, and profession;
* heroic without becoming theatrical;
* fantastical without becoming visually excessive;
* historically influenced without reproducing one specific real-world culture;
* asymmetrical, layered, and functional;
* designed for travel, climate, combat, ritual, labor, or court life as appropriate;
* socially coded through silhouette, material quality, wear, heraldry, and construction;
* appropriate for a world where magic, religion, monsters, politics, trade, and ancestry overlap.

Favor:

* tunics, robes, cloaks, mantles, tabards, gambesons, fitted jackets, and layered travel garments;

* decorative equipment that does not support role, history, culture, or personality.

The costume must remain visually subordinate to the face.

## Facial Construction

* **Geometric Foundation:** Construct the face from broad angular planes rather than smooth rounded modeling.

* **Plane Reduction:** Divide the forehead, temples, cheekbones, nose, mouth area, jaw, and neck into a limited number of clearly readable shapes.

* **Large Shapes First:** Establish the entire head using large flat masses before adding facial detail.

* **Minimal Blending:** Allow neighboring color planes to remain visibly distinct. Use only restrained soft transitions around the cheeks, eye sockets, and jaw.

* **Stylized Anatomy:** Simplify and subtly exaggerate the head shape, cheekbones, jaw, nose, neck, hands, and limb proportions into a coherent authored design. Favor graphic rhythm and silhouette over naturalistic measurement.

* **Controlled Asymmetry:** Preserve subtle differences between both sides of the face. Avoid perfectly mirrored features.

* **Simplified Features:** Avoid excessive eyelash detail, lip texture, nostril rendering, wrinkles, pores, fine creases, or micro-anatomy.

* **Structural Readability:** The face should remain clearly readable from a distance through light, midtone, and shadow masses.

### Eyes

* The eyes are the primary focal point.
* Use sharp upper eyelids and simplified lower eyelids.
* Keep the iris small and restrained.
* Use one subtle highlight per eye.
* Avoid glossy anime eyes or highly detailed irises.
* Use muted under-eye shadow shapes to communicate fatigue.
* Preserve a guarded, intelligent, distant, observant, or analytical gaze.
* The eyes may carry one restrained cool color accent.

### Nose

* Build the nose from two or three simple planes.
* Use a clear bridge plane, one side shadow, and a minimal underside shadow.
* Avoid detailed nostrils or smooth photorealistic modeling.
* Keep the nose readable through shape contrast rather than linework.

### Mouth

* Use a simplified upper-lip shadow and a single light plane on the lower lip.
* Preserve subtle natural asymmetry.
* Avoid glossy lips, sharp cosmetic edges, excessive creases, or detailed texture.
* Keep the expression controlled, readable, and psychologically specific.

### Neck

* Construct the neck using long vertical or diagonal value shapes.
* Use only a few structural shadows to suggest tendons and form.
* Avoid detailed anatomical rendering.
* Allow the lower neck to merge into the collar or clothing mass.

## Three-Value Facial Structure

The face should be organized around a simplified value system:

1. **Light Plane:** Forehead, upper cheekbone, bridge of the nose, upper eyelid area, and selected jaw surfaces.
2. **Midtone Plane:** Main local skin color covering most of the face.
3. **Shadow Plane:** Eye sockets, side of the nose, underside of the cheekbone, jaw, under-lip area, and neck.
4. **Deep Accent:** Eyelashes, nostril indication, mouth separation, eyebrow accents, hair contact shadows, and a few selected facial edges.

Do not introduce many intermediate shades.

Do not blend all planes into smooth gradients.

The geometry of the light and shadow must remain visible.

The face should feel sculpted through broad value blocks rather than modeled through continuous softness.

## Brushwork Rules

* **Primary Brush:** Use a broad, flat, semi-opaque digital brush with a slightly dry or chalky edge.

* **Secondary Brush:** Use a smaller textured brush only for the eyes, mouth, eyebrows, selected hair accents, important costume edges, and one or two environmental edges.

* **Stroke Size:** Favor medium and large strokes over small strokes.

* **Stroke Direction:** Follow the structural planes of the face, neck, hair, clothing, architecture, furniture masses, and atmospheric shapes.

* **Stroke Count:** Keep the number of visible strokes limited and purposeful.

* **Opacity:** Use mostly opaque strokes with occasional semi-transparent corrections.

* **Texture:** Use only subtle brush grain. Texture must never overpower the geometric shapes.

* **Blending:** Blend sparingly. Preserve visible boundaries between major tonal planes.

* **Corrections:** Allow overlapping strokes, dry edges, and slight irregularities rather than smoothing every transition.

* **Surface Logic:** Each visible stroke should describe form, direction, material, atmosphere, or edge hierarchy.

The surface should appear painted but not heavily textured.

The character illustration must feel controlled, economical, graphic, and intentionally designed rather than loose, chaotic, impressionistic, or naturalistically rendered.

Do not outline the entire environment.

## Material Simplification

Differentiate materials primarily through shape, value, edge quality, and only a few restrained highlights.

* **Skin:** Flat and semi-flat tonal planes with subtle dry-brush transitions.
* **Hair:** Large matte shapes with restrained directional highlights.
* **Heavy Wool:** Broad matte masses with minimal fold structure.
* **Linen and Cotton:** Soft cream or grey-beige planes with restrained wrinkles.
* **Leather:** Dark matte shapes with one or two broken highlights and slightly worn edges.
* **Fur:** One broad soft-edged mass with minimal directional texture.
* **Padded Cloth:** Broad quilted or layered masses suggested by only a few structural divisions.
* **Chain or Scale:** A unified mid-value metal mass with sparse broken highlights rather than individually rendered links or scales.
* **Plate Armor:** Large dull geometric planes with restrained edge wear and no mirror-like reflections.
* **Fine Court Fabric:** Slightly smoother planes, restrained edge highlights, and subtle tonal richness without visible pattern complexity.
* **Metal:** Oxidized iron, dull steel, tarnished silver, dark bronze, or blackened metal accents.
* **Glass:** Small muted reflections only.
* **Stone and Plaster:** Broad matte planes with very restrained surface variation.
* **Wood:** Large dark or warm rectangular masses with minimal grain indication.
* **Bone, Horn, and Antler:** Warm matte shapes with minimal tonal variation and no decorative excess.
* **White Fabric:** Warm cream, aged ivory, smoke-grey, or muted beige rather than pure white.
* **Black Fabric:** Charcoal, brown-black, muted indigo, deep green-grey, or smoky umber rather than absolute black.

Avoid glossy photorealistic surfaces, polished fantasy armor, bright gold ornament, chrome-like reflections, individually rendered chain links, excessive fabric texture, and realistic masonry.

* Let the light clarify the cheekbones, nose bridge, eyes, jaw, collar, major garment planes, and primary environmental anchor.
* The environmental light must visually connect the character to the location.
* A subtle secondary ambient or rim light may separate one moving edge of the silhouette, but it must remain subordinate.
* Allow one controlled rim-light accent along the action-facing edge of the body, weapon, cloak, or hair.
* Allow restrained cinematic emphasis without broad bloom or glossy rendering.
* Avoid multiple bright colored light sources.
* Avoid complex reflected lighting.
* Avoid tiny highlights distributed across the image.
* Avoid theatrical backlighting that obscures the face or turns the figure into a silhouette.
* Avoid glossy facial highlights.

The lighting should clarify the face, action, costume, and location while adding focused splash-art impact without becoming visual spectacle.

## Atmospheric Treatment

Use one restrained atmospheric effect to unify the character, action, and location:

* soft woodland, mountain, coastal, subterranean, or battlefield haze;
* muted dust;
* broad smoke masses;
* diffused window light;
* gentle shadow gradients;
* subtle worn-wall texture;
* restrained grain;
* faded color transitions;
* lost edges where clothing meets the environment;
* soft value shifts between foreground and background;
* limited atmospheric perspective.
* one directional trail or arc supporting the principal gesture.

Atmosphere must be painted as large soft masses.

Do not use:

* dense or uniformly distributed particles;
* individually rendered dust specks;
* decorative magical sparkles unrelated to the action;
* rain droplets;
* multiple elaborate smoke effects;
* dense fog covering the character;
* glowing debris;
* numerous floating objects;
* environmental motion moving in several conflicting directions.

## Composition

* **Format:** Vertical 9:16.
* **Framing:** Full-body character illustration, showing the complete figure from the top of the head to the soles of the boots.
* **Character Scale:** The complete character occupies approximately 75–85% of the image height.
* **Pose:** A controlled action pose captured at the beginning, midpoint, or aftermath of one role-specific movement.
* **Silhouette:** Simple, expressive, socially coded, and identifiable, with one dominant diagonal or curved action line.
* **Focal Point:** Eyes and central facial features.
* **Secondary Focus:** The complete action silhouette, hands, role-defining equipment, hair, clothing flow, and torso rotation.
* **Tertiary Focus:** One restrained narrative effect, meaningful symbol, footwear shape, or environmental anchor.
* **Low-Priority Areas:** Minor garment folds, secondary boot details, distant architecture, and peripheral scenery.
* **Negative Space:** Uncluttered and asymmetrically balanced around the direction of movement.
* **Perspective:** Natural eye-level or moderately low three-quarter view, without extreme lens distortion.
* **Cropping:** Do not crop the head, hair, shoulders, elbows, hands, equipment, garment hems, legs, ankles, footwear, or feet. Preserve a small visible margin beneath the lowest foot.
* **Environment Placement:** The location should frame and support the figure without obscuring the silhouette.

Avoid partial-body framing, uncontrolled action, extreme perspective, severe foreshortening, unreadably wide gestures, theatrical acrobatics, or complex battle-scene framing.

---

## Pose and Action Direction

Favor controlled action beats such as:

* advancing one step while beginning to draw or lower a weapon;
* turning the torso while raising a shield, focus, book, tool, or ritual object;
* finishing a compact strike, defensive movement, spell, prayer, performance, or alchemical action;
* tracking something outside the frame while cloak, hair, or loose fabric follows the turn;
* shifting weight between clearly readable legs while one foot remains grounded;
* using one arm as the leading gesture and the other to stabilize equipment or silhouette;
* allowing one cloak edge, sash, braid, smoke trail, leaf arc, spark trail, or magical ribbon to echo the main action line;
* preserving clear hand, weapon, leg, and footwear shapes throughout the movement.

Avoid:

* acrobatic or gravity-defying poses;
* exaggerated contrapposto unrelated to the action;
* chaotic multi-action combat poses;
* theatrical hand gestures without role-specific purpose;
* fabric moving independently of wind or body motion;
* full sprinting or high leaping that makes the complete figure difficult to frame;
* weapons raised toward the viewer;
* extreme facial or body movement.

The pose should communicate personality, class, and narrative through one controlled moment of movement.

## Detail Distribution

Follow this strict detail distribution:

* **Face and eyes:** 100% detail.
* **Hair near the face:** 70% detail.
* **Leading hand, face-framing hair, and action-facing edges:** 65% detail.
* **Secondary hand, neck, and collar:** 50% detail.
* **Primary upper garment structure:** 45% detail.
* **Role-defining symbol, focus, tool, shield, or weapon:** 45% detail.
* **Chest rotation, torso, armor, and upper garment layers:** 40% detail.
* **Single narrative effect near the action:** 30% detail.
* **Primary environmental anchor:** 25% detail.
* **Lower garments, legs, and major lower-body shapes:** 25% detail.
* **Footwear and readable weight placement:** 22% detail.
* **Secondary environmental cues:** 15% detail.
* **Outer silhouette and peripheral garment edges:** 12% detail.
* **Distant environment:** 8% detail.
* **Peripheral background:** 5% detail.

Do not apply equal detail across the entire image.

The reduction of detail away from the face and principal action must be clearly visible.

The clothing must remain legible without becoming intricately rendered.

The environment must remain recognizable without becoming fully illustrated.

## Final Visual Target

The final image should resemble a stylized professional full-body character splash illustration created for a mature medieval-fantasy role-playing game, heroic adventure, political fantasy, ancient mystery, or dark folklore setting with its own authored visual identity.

It must feel:

* geometric;
* shape-driven;
* unmistakably stylized rather than realistic;
* controlled but energetic;
* intelligent;
* economical;
* subtly painterly;
* emotionally observant;
* deliberately unfinished;
* visually simple;
* socially grounded;
* fantastical but grounded;
* elegant but worn;
* environmentally recognizable;
* atmospheric but controlled;
* original rather than derivative;
* focused on the face and principal action.

The complete body must be part of the design statement. The pose, costume silhouette, limbs, role-defining equipment, and footwear should read as one cohesive moving full-body shape, with visibly stylized proportions and clear graphic rhythm. At least one foot should visibly carry or transfer weight, and both feet must remain readable rather than fading into the background.

The image should capture one narrative instant: the anticipation, midpoint, or aftermath of a single role-specific action. A diagonal body gesture and one controlled trail of cloth, hair, dust, leaves, ash, sparks, mist, or magic should create splash-art energy without becoming a complete combat scene.

The costume design should suggest an original medieval-fantasy world defined by:

* rival kingdoms and city-states;
* ancient ruins and forgotten powers;
* feudal duties and contested faiths;
* dangerous wilderness and monsters;
* arcane, divine, and primal traditions;
* cultural exchange and regional craft;
* strong class divisions and political conflict;
* adventuring, pilgrimage, trade, and war;
* practical survival;
* restrained wonder.

The environment should feel like a location remembered through impression, structure, color, light, and atmosphere rather than observed with photographic precision.

The viewer should understand where the character is without being invited to inspect the location in detail.

The clothing must remain graphically simple, with identity conveyed through silhouette, layering, proportion, wear, material contrast, and a small number of meaningful details rather than exhaustive ornamentation.

The final result must prioritize:

* large readable shapes;
* angular facial and body planes;
* simplified, authored game-art proportions;
* one clear diagonal or curved action line;
* three-value construction;
* limited color transitions;
* confident visible brushwork;
* minimal blending;
* simplified hair masses;
* culturally grounded medieval-fantasy costume silhouettes;
* abstract clothing shapes;
* socially readable tailoring;
* a recognizable but incomplete location;
* one dominant environmental anchor;
* minimal contextual props;
* one restrained narrative effect tied to the action;
* selective sharp edges;
* soft outer contours;
* controlled color harmony with one moderately stronger accent;
* atmospheric integration between figure and environment;
* a strong contrast between the resolved face and action area and the simplified but fully readable body and background;
* and an uncropped head-to-toe action silhouette with visible footwear and readable weight placement.

The image should look as though it was created with a small number of deliberate brushstrokes, not through exhaustive rendering.
