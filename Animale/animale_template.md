## Technical Rendering Engine (STRICT & FIXED — DO NOT MODIFY)

Create a high-quality anime fantasy RPG character splash illustration rendered as mature, dramatic, semi-painterly anime key art.

The image must combine:

* selective expressive anime linework;
* deep graphic shadow masses;
* jewel-toned but controlled color harmony;
* large cel-shaped values with selective painterly transitions;
* mature authored facial construction;
* localized chromatic illumination;
* medium detail density;
* large readable shapes;
* simplified ornaments;
* grouped accessories;
* controlled focal detail;
* strong character-background separation.

The character must feel designed for an original fantasy game rather than copied from an existing anime, game, franchise, artist, studio, or character.

The result should be decisive, graceful, moody, chromatic, readable, and fantasy-rich without becoming hyper-detailed, generically glossy, or visually flat.

The authorial identity comes from the relationship between deep shadow, selective linework, mature anime facial anatomy, richly grouped hair, shaped local light, and a sharply resolved focal area.

## Color System — 60/30/10

Adhere to the user-defined palette:

* **Primary — 60%:** Deep base family unifying clothing, hair, large shadow masses, and atmosphere.
* **Secondary — 30%:** Chromatic supporting family used across outfit planes, reflected light, equipment, and narrative background silhouettes.
* **Accent — 10%:** Highly focused luminous color used on eyes, magic, elemental effects, a weapon edge, instrument, jewelry, or another focal element.

Use moody, jewel-toned anime color harmony with clear chromatic structure.

Favor deep colored shadows, controlled saturation, and clear hue separation over muddy greys, pastel defaults, or uniformly bright fantasy color.

Keep shadows chromatic and substantial. Near-black masses may be used when they retain a subtle blue, violet, green, red, or umber bias.

Use gradients only inside selected painterly transitions, reflected light, atmosphere, or emissive glow. Preserve large readable value shapes.

### Character-Background Separation

Never place the same value and saturation on adjacent character and background areas.

When a primary color appears in both layers, change at least one of:

* value;
* saturation;
* temperature;
* edge sharpness;
* rim lighting.

If the character is light, place a deeper, cooler, warmer, or less saturated mass behind the silhouette.

If the character is dark, use a lighter background mass or restrained rim light.

Prioritize value separation first and color harmony second.

The face, hair, hands, torso silhouette, and principal gesture must remain clearly separated from the background, even when they share the same dark color family.

## Lighting and Focal Glow

Use one principal ambient light and one localized emissive or shaped focal light.

The ambient light establishes the environment and broad shadow masses.

The focal light may come from:

* magic;
* weapon;
* instrument;
* artifact;
* moonlight;
* stained glass;
* flame;
* crystal;
* another narratively justified source.

Let the focal light interact with nearby anatomy and materials:

* underlight or side-light the face selectively;
* catch the lower eyelids or irises without making the eyes uniformly glossy;
* illuminate the nearest fingers and palm planes;
* create colored reflections in nearby hair masses;
* reveal selected garment folds and equipment edges;
* fade rapidly outside the focal region.

The light source, hands, face, and direction of gaze should form one connected visual circuit.

Allow saturated cyan, violet, magenta, emerald, red, amber, or another palette-specific light when it remains localized.

Use a single rim-light passage only where silhouette separation requires it.

Avoid:

* uniform frontal beauty lighting;
* rim light surrounding the entire character;
* ambient glow applied equally to every surface;
* multiple equally bright emissive sources;
* white bloom erasing color;
* light covering facial anatomy;
* glow replacing hand structure;
* generic blue-and-magenta lighting when unrelated to the chosen palette.

## Composition System

* **Rule:** Dynamic anime RPG key-art asymmetry.
* **Aspect Ratio:** Vertical 9:16.
* **Framing:** Tight extended upper-body composition from the top of the head to just below the waist or across the upper hips. Keep both focal hands and the principal weapon, instrument, artifact, or power source fully visible. Expand toward the upper thighs only when required to preserve the action or prop.
* **Character Scale:** The character generally occupies 88–95% of the image height, while leaving enough breathing room for hair, hands, equipment, and focal glow.
* **Placement:** Choose left third, right third, or near-center asymmetry according to pose direction, gaze, silhouette, weapon, instrument, magic flow, and background needs.
* **Camera:** Dramatic eye level or slight low angle without extreme perspective distortion.
* **Focal Hierarchy:** Face and eyes first; hands, weapon, instrument, or power source second; costume motifs third; background last.
* **Negative Space:** Preserve clean space around the face, hands, hair silhouette, elbows, waist, and main gesture.
* **Depth:** Maintain clear foreground, character, and background layers.

Do not default every character to the same side.

Balance the character with one dominant designed background countershape placed according to the pose, gaze, and action line.

Use diagonals, triangular structures, cape flow, hair movement, fabric, magic, or symbolic forms to lead the eye back to the face.

Avoid:

* centered passport composition;
* extreme foreshortening;
* complex lens distortion;
* cropped head or hands;
* hidden hips;
* framing ending above the waist;
* unnecessary pullback to mid-thigh when the pose and prop already fit near the upper hips;
* visible knees or full-body framing;
* background shapes tangent to facial or hand contours.

## Anime Rendering Standard

* **Style:** Dramatic semi-painterly anime fantasy key art with mature facial construction and a concentrated luminous focus.
* **Lineart:** Selective expressive anime linework with variable weight, strongest around eyes, facial accents, fingers, and key equipment; reduced, colored, broken, or lost inside hair, deep shadow, fabric, and atmosphere.
* **Shading:** Large graphic cel-shaped value masses combined with painterly chromatic transitions only where they describe face, hair, hands, material, or reflected light.
* **Lighting:** Deep ambient shadow structure with one shaped local light source casting jewel-toned illumination across the focal area.
* **Edges:** Crisp selected focal edges, medium costume and anatomy edges, and soft or dissolved atmospheric edges.
* **Textures:** Subtle digital paint grain and material variation without pores, grit, or dense surface noise.
* **Brushwork:** Controlled semi-painterly finish with visible color decisions and slight irregularity rather than uniformly smooth mobile-game polish.

The image should look illustrated, not rendered in 3D.

Avoid:

* 3D render;
* CGI;
* Unreal Engine appearance;
* Octane Render appearance;
* ray-traced surfaces;
* photorealism;
* gritty realism;
* generic dark-lord styling;
* western adult animation;
* dead neutral black without chromatic bias;
* muddy colors;
* horror treatment when not requested;
* depth-of-field blur;
* volumetric fog.
