## Lighting and Layered Painterly Shadows

### Value Families

Organize the painting into a light family, a midtone family, and a shadow family, supported by selective deep accents. These are broad value groups, not a restriction to three literal colors.

Allow restrained value and temperature variations within each family. They should reveal secondary planes without breaking the overall light-and-shadow separation. A painted patch inside shadow remains recognizably part of shadow; do not turn every plane change into a highlight.

Keep the face's key features at the strongest local contrast. Support them with readable head and torso midtones. Darker lower garments and peripheral masses may anchor the image, but do not bury the character's construction in black.

### Shadow Structure

* **Primary form shadow:** Establish a broad, connected shadow group on planes turning away from the light. Keep its silhouette intentional and expressive.
* **Secondary form planes:** Add selected darker or lighter painted shapes within that group to describe cheek structure, the turn of an arm, a folded sleeve, or a garment bending around the torso.
* **Cast shadows:** Show purposeful shadows where one form blocks the light: hair on forehead or cheek, chin on neck, collar on chest, sleeve on arm, a hand on clothing, or a belt over trousers. Use only relationships actually present in the specified pose.
* **Contact accents:** Place small, deep shadows at important close overlaps and recesses. Avoid an automatic dark outline around every object or joint.

Shadow richness comes from the design of these shapes, their relative values, and their placement. Keep asymmetrical cuts and secondary planes where they explain the subject; leave other areas broad and quiet.

### Dark-Area Readability

Preserve differences among hair, fabric, leather, and background even when all are dark. Use restrained shifts in value, temperature, edge, and plane direction. The shared cool shadow family may contain multiple related mixtures suited to each local material and skin tone.

A subtle environmental influence may appear as a muted painted plane inside shadow, but it must not create a second dramatic light source, a bright rim, or a photographic bounce-light simulation.

### Shadow Edges

Use decisive edges for selected cast shadows and close contacts, more painterly edges for broad form turns, and limited soft transitions where adjacent planes need integration. Do not make every shadow razor-sharp or equally soft. Preserve visible plane boundaries across the image.

Avoid uniform dark fills, smooth airbrushed modeling, glossy facial highlights, theatrical backlighting, multiple colored lights, bloom, and scattered tiny highlights. More elaborate shadows must not turn into photographic rendering or a dense polygon mosaic.

## Brushwork and Edge Hierarchy
